# Parent guide

This is the text of the Guide section in the app's parent zone (FR-33 to FR-37). It has 8 short cards, each ending with one thing to try this week. The app shows one as the "Tip of the week", and parents can read the rest any time. The machine-readable version is [`content/guide.json`](../content/guide.json).

## 1. Why they understand but don't speak

Your children hear enough Portuguese to understand it, but not enough to feel at ease saying it. Research on bilingual children shows that without enough of a language, children can learn it "but do not reach a comfort level in the language so that they will willingly use it" [1]. They also need a bigger share of their input in the minority language than in English to keep up [1]. Understanding comes from listening. Speaking comes from practice at finding the words and saying them, which is the part this app and you can add.

**Try this week:** notice how often each child says something in Portuguese to you without being asked, and log it with the + button. That's your starting point.

## 2. The biggest lever is you sticking to Portuguese

A study of close to 2,000 families found that when one parent speaks only the majority language, 74% of children spoke the minority language if the other parent used it consistently. If that parent also spoke the majority language to the children, only 36% did [2][3]. In the same study, 94% of children spoke it when one parent used the minority language and the other parent spoke both [3]. That means Mum using even a few Portuguese words and phrases helps too. The numbers show a strong link, not proof of cause, but the pattern is clear: the more consistently Portuguese is used at home, the more likely children are to speak it.

**Try this week:** pick one daily moment where you never switch to English, such as breakfast, the school run or bedtime. Mum can join in with "Bom dia!" and "Obrigada".

## 3. When they answer you in English

Parents respond in a few typical ways. They can carry on in their own language and accept the English ("move on"). They can say what the child meant in Portuguese ("adult repetition"). They can guess what the child meant, as a question ("expressed guess"), or say they didn't understand ("minimal grasp") [4][5]. A study of fathers with children who understood but rarely spoke their language found the fathers carried on about 93% of the time. The occasional prompt to switch languages had little effect on its own [5]. Nudges work when they're a steady habit, not a rare event, and when the child has a real reason to speak.

- **Say it back:** "Queres água? Diz: quero água." Then wait.
- **Guess, as a question:** "Queres ir ao parque? Diz-me em português."
- **Don't understand, on purpose:** "Como? Não percebi…" Use it lightly and at most once or twice, then give them the words. It should feel like a game, not a test.

**Try this week:** every time they answer in English, say it back in Portuguese and pause for three seconds before carrying on.

## 4. Say it back, don't correct

When a child says "Este é a mãe", don't answer "No, it's esta". Say it back correctly as part of your natural reply: "Ah, esta é a mãe! Olá, mãe!" This is the same technique as adult repetition in card 3 [4][5]. The conversation keeps going, the child hears the right form straight away, and nobody feels they got it wrong. The app does the same thing: Gui repeats the phrase correctly and the game carries on.

**Try this week:** catch yourself before saying "no" or "wrong" to a Portuguese attempt. Praise that they tried, and say it back correctly.

## 5. Your weekly plan

Aim for about 3–4 hours a week of situations where the children are expected to speak Portuguese, plus as much Portuguese around them as you can manage. The app is only a small part of that. Its main job is to create reasons for them to speak to you. Short and daily works better than long and weekly.

| Activity | Time per week | What it builds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fala Comigo sessions (2 × 12 min) | ~25 min | Speaking practice, new phrases |
| Missions to Dad (daily, ~5 min) | ~35 min | Speaking to you in real life |
| Portuguese-only routine with Dad, e.g. dinner or bedtime, 4–5 times | ~1.5 h | Real conversation |
| A Portuguese outing or game once a week | ~1 h | Speaking in a new setting |
| Video calls with the avós (2 × 15 min) | ~30 min | A real reason to speak |
| Cartoons, films and audiobooks in European Portuguese | 3–5 h | Listening, sounding natural |

These times are a practical starting point, not a research-backed minimum. The research shows that more input helps, but doesn't give a single number that guarantees speaking [1][6].

**Try this week:** put the Portuguese-only routine and the two app sessions on the family calendar.

## 6. Keep it fun, never a punishment

*Practical advice.* Children avoid a language that feels like a test or a chore. So keep the stakes low. Never take away screen time or treats for speaking English, and don't make them perform Portuguese for visitors. Celebrate every attempt, including half-English sentences. The app follows the same rules: no punishing streaks, no "wrong" buzzers, and a missed day just gets "Tive saudades!" from Gui.

**Try this week:** make up a Portuguese-only silly game, such as "O Pai diz…" (Simon says) or "Eu vejo…" (I spy).

## 7. Give them people to speak to

*Practical advice.* Children speak the language that works with the people who matter to them. Grandparents, cousins and Portuguese friends who don't switch to English give the language a real purpose that no app can. Time in Portugal, where Portuguese is the only thing that works, is the strongest version of this. Before a trip, play Unit 7 (the grandparents' village) and practise the phrases they'll need there.

**Try this week:** ask the avós to stick to Portuguese on calls, even if it's slow, and to ask the children one simple question each time.

## 8. What progress looks like

Understanding will stay ahead of speaking for a long time. That's normal. Expect roughly these stages:

1. Portuguese words dropped into English sentences ("Can I have a gelado?"). Mixing languages is a normal part of growing up bilingual, not a sign of confusion [4].
2. Set phrases used in the right situation ("Posso ter…?", "Boa noite, pai!").
3. Short sentences they build themselves, with mistakes.
4. Short back-and-forth conversations in Portuguese.

The number to watch is the unprompted Portuguese counter on the Overview screen, not coins or levels. If it goes up month by month, it's working.

**Try this week:** compare this week's counter with last week's, and tell the children you noticed. Praise matters.
